Region Guard admin screen: three options

Each mock-up is one Minecraft screen at GUI scale 2 (854 by 480 pixels), drawn in BSP Core's Holo HUD style: dark panel, one-pixel turquoise border, upper-case title at top left, tabs in the header. The same seven example regions appear in all three: spawn, market, arena, mine_iron, plot_a1 (a claim owned by Mrgregles), plots_north (open for buying) and the world default. Pick one, or name the parts you want combined.

A. Ledger list on the left, one region's detail on the right

The classic two-pane admin screen. A searchable list with type filters on the left; the chosen region's tabs on the right (FLAGS, ACCESS, RANKS, SALE, MINE, TELEPORT, INFO). Flags are a scrolling table grouped by WorldGuard's categories, each with an inherit / allow / deny switch and a search box, so the full set of about ninety flags stays workable. Where the region is in the world is a line of text, not a picture.

Good at: many regions and many flags; keyboard-friendly; the fastest to build and the easiest to extend with a new tab per feature. Weak at: seeing where regions are and how they overlap; you still rely on the wand and the in-world drawing for shape.

B. Map a top-down map of the world with regions drawn on it

The map is the screen. Built from the chunk heightmap the way BSP Core's chunk picker is, with every region drawn as a tinted box in its type's colour and labelled. Click a region to open the drawer on the right with its summary and the flags it sets; double-click to open the full editor (option A's tabs, in a second screen). Drag on empty ground to draw a new region without the wand. The bottom bar switches world, toggles layers (protected, claims, mines, for sale) and zooms.

Good at: seeing overlaps, gaps and parents at a glance; drawing plots for sale; spotting a claim where it should not be. Weak at: tall regions and vertical layering (the map is flat); needs the client to know region shapes, so it comes with more network code; a second screen is still needed for the long flag table.

C. Inspector one region at a time, flags as a card grid, a map thumbnail in the corner

A single-pane editor built around the region you are standing in. The left rail switches sections (flags, people, ranks, sale, mine, teleport, info); the header shows the region with previous and next arrows and a search box. Flags are cards in a grid, grouped by category, each with its switch and a one-line hint, so a tab shows twelve or so at once without a table. The right column keeps a small map thumbnail with the region highlighted plus the key facts and the allowed ranks.

Good at: editing one region quickly in the field: open it with the wand while standing inside, change three flags, close; reads well on a laptop screen; the card hints teach new admins what a flag does. Weak at: comparing many regions; the card grid takes more room per flag than a table, so more scrolling for the full set.

Chosen: A with a MAP tab the ledger layout; the map is the first tab of the detail pane

The owner's pick (2026-10-11): A's two panes and its flag table, with B's map folded into the right pane as the first tab. The screen opens on MAP with the selected region highlighted and its neighbours drawn in their type colours; click a box on the map to select that region in the list, double-click to jump to its FLAGS tab. The drawer, layer chips and zoom bar from B are gone. Drawing a region on the map is not in the first version; the wand and "new from selection" do that.

Opens: /rg gui, or sneak and right-click with the wand. Players: a cut-down version for their own claims comes with Phase 4. Hints: the short grey flag explanations stay on the screen and hide when the window is small.
