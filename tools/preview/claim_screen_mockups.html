Claim screen for players: three options

What a player sees for their own claims (owner decision D17). Buying stays in BSP Core's shop screen, so this screen is for living with a claim: who may come in and build, the handful of flags an owner may set (the config list: entry, chest access, use, interact, ride, sleep, greeting, farewell, entry message, mob spawning, PvP), facts, and giving the claim up. Same Holo HUD style as the admin screen. Example player: Kai, with two claims, field-kai-1 (outlined in the open area "field") and shop3 (an admin plot). Opened with /claim gui or by sneaking and right-clicking the plot tool.

A. Small ledger the admin screen's layout, cut down to what a player needs

My claims on the left (and, below them, plots for sale and open areas nearby, each with a "buy in the shop" hint). The chosen claim on the right with four tabs: MAP, PEOPLE, FLAGS, INFO. The flag tab is the same switch row as the admin screen but only for the allowed flags. Players who have seen an admin at work will find it familiar, and it shares nearly all its code with the admin screen.

Good at: several claims; one code base. Weak at: it looks like a tool, not a home screen; for a single claim the list column is wasted space.

B. Claim card one claim at a time, map first, everything on one page

Opens on the claim you are standing in (or your first). A map of the claim and its neighbours on the left, with the claim outlined and the plots for sale around it in gold, so enlarging and buying next door make sense visually. On the right, members as chips with a + and an x, and the allowed flags as simple on/off switches with one-line hints (inherit is "follow the area's rule", shown as a third, grey state). Facts and the GIVE UP button at the bottom. Arrows in the header step through your claims.

Good at: the usual case (one or two claims); nothing to learn; the map explains what a claim is. Weak at: many claims (arrows only); a long allowed-flag list would need scrolling.

C. Compact panel a small inventory-sized panel, no map, switches only

A 300 by 180 panel like a chest screen, for quick changes without leaving the game: the claim's name and size, a row of member heads with + and x, the allowed flags as two columns of switches, and GIVE UP. Everything the owner can change is one click away and the panel never scrolls; a tab row at the top switches between claims. No map: the in-world drawing (the violet box) already shows the claim.

Good at: speed and small screens; nothing to explain. Weak at: text flags (greeting, refusal message) need a separate line; no room for plots for sale; no map for enlarging.

How to answer

Reply with A, B or C, or a mix. My recommendation is B: a player has one or two claims, the map makes enlarging and buying next door obvious, and the on/off wording ("strangers may enter") is easier than inherit / allow / deny. If the allowed-flag list grows past about ten, B's rules area scrolls.

  • Opening: /claim gui and sneak + right-click with the plot tool (the same wand), unless you prefer something else.
  • Wording: the flags are shown as plain sentences on this screen ("strangers may open chests") rather than flag names; the command keeps the names.
  • Teleport home: a "TELEPORT" button to your claim is cheap to add; say if claims should not be teleport targets (BSP-Teleport may own that later).
