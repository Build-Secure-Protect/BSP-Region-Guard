Edge links between servers: three options

The world border lives on the world default (the global region), so its controls get a new BORDER tab when the world default is selected in the admin screen. The same tab links each edge of this world to an edge of a world on another server (owner decisions D40 and D41): links are two-way, a player arrives at the same fraction along the other edge, 16 blocks inside it. At a linked edge the player gets a pop-up asking whether to travel. Example network: this server survival-1 (Overworld, 10,000 wide around 0, 0) and the others Region Guard knows from the shared storage: survival-2 (10,000), resource (8,000), creative (4,000) and lobby (1,000). East is already linked to survival-2's west edge, north to resource's south edge.

A. Map board the worlds as squares; click an edge, then the edge it leads to

This world sits in the middle of a board with the linked worlds docked against their edges, drawn to scale against each other. Worlds not yet linked wait in a tray underneath. To link, click a free edge of this world (it turns gold), then an edge of a world in the tray; to unlink, click a link and press UNLINK. The border's width and centre sit above the board.

Good at: showing the shape of the whole network at a glance; sizes are obvious, so a 10,000 edge leading to a 4,000 edge reads as a squeeze. Weak at: exact numbers (where exactly you arrive) need a hover; a network of many servers gets crowded.

B. Edge table one row per edge, picked from a list

The four edges as rows: how long each is, where it leads and where a player crossing its middle arrives. LINK opens a list of every free edge on the other servers, with its length; picking one links both ways. Edges of different lengths show the stretch ("x 0.4") so the admin sees that the far side is shorter.

Good at: precise; quick to scan; easy to build and to read on a small window. Weak at: no picture of how the worlds fit together; easier to link the wrong pair of edges by mistake.

C. Compass this world in the middle, one neighbour slot per side

This world as a tile in the centre with a slot on each side, like a map of a continent split across servers. A filled slot names the server, its world, the edge you arrive on and its size; an empty slot says + LINK. Clicking a slot lists the worlds that still have a free edge on the matching side (for this world's south, their north edge first). The border's size sits in the centre tile.

Good at: reads like a map; the right edge pairs are suggested (south goes to north), so the usual mistakes are hard to make. Weak at: exact arrival points need the slot's detail; sizes are written, not drawn to scale.

WHAT HAPPENS AT A LINKED EDGE (the same in every option)

  1. 10 blocks before the edge the player sees the warning on the action bar, as now.
  2. At the edge they are stopped and the pop-up opens. The wall in the Overworld is Minecraft's; elsewhere the edge is invisible.
  3. TRAVEL: Region Guard works out the arrival point (same fraction along the other edge, 16 blocks inside, on standing ground) and hands the move to BSP Teleport, which sends the player through the proxy.
  4. STAY (or Esc): the pop-up closes, the player is pushed back a little and is not asked again until they have walked out of the 10-block warning band and come back.
  5. Arriving on the other server: they land inside its warning band, so its edge does not ask them to go straight back.
  6. An unlinked edge behaves as today: pushed back with the edge message, no pop-up.
